# Project review and prototype interpretation

The source folder contains 49 course and assignment files and no application checkout. All files were inventoried and text extracted where possible. The SRS, assignment brief, rubric, task-description papers, tutorials, and embedded workflow diagrams were inspected for product requirements. Long architecture textbooks were reviewed through their structure and relevant passages; this is not a claim that every textbook page was read line by line. The two image-only assignment screenshots were rendered and visually inspected. PDF and Word copies frequently duplicate the same content.

## What drives this prototype

The assignment case is a tourism information system commissioned by a local tourism association. It centralizes attractions, accommodation, transportation, food, and other useful visitor information. Management maintains information and databases. The SRS further names itinerary planning and tourism activity reporting as goals.

The SRS uses Lauesen's Tasks and Support approach. Its example-solution column describes possible support, not mandatory screen designs. This app is one candidate solution that demonstrates those tasks; it does not replace the domain-level SRS, invent client validation, or constitute the Assignment 01 submission.

## Source findings

- Completed task descriptions: explore destinations, find/arrange accommodation, find/arrange transportation.
- The attraction and accommodation workflow diagrams are embedded images in the DOCX. They include no-result recovery, comparison, optional itinerary creation, and external-provider handoff. These are represented in the prototype.
- Tasks 4–8, much of scope/context, data-model content, three quality sections, CRUD validation, and possible solutions remain placeholders in the supplied draft.
- Transportation variants appear copied from accommodation. The prototype interprets them as transport search, incomplete operator information, unavailable route, and external arrangements. This needs confirmation in the SRS.
- The appendix discusses bookings as external-provider redirects. The prototype does not take payment or create reservations.
- Staff-only management and a 15-minute idle session limit are specified. The public local management demo illustrates editing and idle expiry without claiming production authorization. A real management service must authenticate and authorize on the server.
- The assignment rubric assesses domain requirements, models, workflows, quality attributes, and validation. A polished UI is supplementary evidence, not proof that those written requirements have been completed or validated with stakeholders.
- The later assignment screenshots concern object design and implementation; they inform future maintainability and demonstration needs without changing Assignment 01's scope.

## Explicit design assumptions

Food discovery, saved favourites, day-by-day itinerary editing, listing maintenance, and browser-local activity reports are inferred from the SRS goals and scope. They are not fabricated completed task descriptions. Browsing requires no account. A responsive website offers desktop and mobile experiences from the same shareable link. Prototype content has 8 attractions, 3 fictional stay examples, and 3 food-discovery entries across Kuching and Miri. Travel durations are planning suggestions, not schedules. Accommodation inventory, prices, transport schedules, opening hours, and booking confirmations are not invented.

Local storage is enough for this UI/UX evaluation. Supabase was not provisioned: it would add shared data/authentication requirements beyond this prototype. Itinerary links encode only the trip data shown to the visitor; recipients explicitly import a copy. Staff-demo modifications do not affect other visitors. No source course documents, student details, IDs, private contact information, or third-party textbook content are included in the application or repository.
